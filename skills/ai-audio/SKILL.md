# AI Audio: Voice, Music & Restoration

AI audio covers generation (voice/music), separation (stems), restoration (denoise/de-reverb), and translation (multilingual dubbing). Legal frameworks are still forming — platform terms ≠ legal rights.

## Three iron rules
1. A voice is an identity asset: cloning a real person's voice is like using their image/name — explicit consent + authorization + disclosure required.
2. Pure AI-generated works are generally not copyrightable (US Copyright Office): "you own it" on the platform does not mean you can claim copyright.
3. Disclosure is the floor: anything that could be mistaken for a real recording must be labeled as AI.

## Voice cloning (four principles)
1. Explicit consent: specific (use case defined) + informed (understands it is AI) + written record. Verbal/default consent does not count.
2. License terms: consent ≠ unlimited use — specify channels, duration, territory, reuse/modification rights, and what happens after employment ends.
3. Disclosure: must label when content could be mistaken for a real recording (ads, customer service, finance/medical contexts especially).
4. Platform and local rules: platform synthetic-media policies are often stricter and more current than law.

Risk by subject: own voice (low, still avoid deceptive use); employees/collaborators (medium, separate written agreement — employment contracts usually do not cover it); professional voice actors (medium, formal license with channels/duration/re-generation/modification); public figures (high, prohibited unless directly authorized); minors (very high, parental consent + low-risk uses, avoid commercial ads); voices "found online" (high — public ≠ usable).

Consent record should include: identity, cloning process description, intended uses, distribution channels, term and renewal, compensation, withdrawal process — archive it like a contract.

## AI music (2026 status)
- RIAA lawsuits against Suno/Udio (training on copyrighted recordings) are reshaping user rights; some majors have settled.
- US Copyright Office (Part 2, 2025): pure AI-generated output (prompt-only) is not copyrightable; "providing prompts" is not human authorship (Thaler v. Perlmutter). Copyright covers only the human contribution (lyrics, composition, perceptible creative arrangement, mixing, human performance); the AI part is public domain.
- Platform terms vs rights gap: paid plans grant commercial USE, but do not guarantee copyright ownership, do not guarantee output uniqueness, and require you to indemnify the platform. You get a license, not exclusive rights.
- Commercial risk layers: no copyright = no exclusivity (licensors require it); training-data infringement risk (grey zone pre-litigation); output similarity lawsuits (and you indemnify the platform); platform/distributor refusal (streamers tighten AI review); disclosure obligations (FTC/platform labeling).

## AI separation & restoration
- Stem separation (Demucs/Spleeter open source; LALAL/UnMixLabs online): vocals/accompaniment/drums/bass from a mix. Quality depends on mix complexity; separating others' works is copyright-constrained (covers/samples need rights).
- Restoration: denoise (AC/hum/wind), de-reverb, de-click, damaged recording repair, dialogue clarity (iZotope RX class). Workflow: restore BEFORE mixing.
- Caution: over-restoration creates artifacts; remove noise in stages, keep the voice natural.

## AI generation application
- AI voiceover workflow: script → AI voice (timbre/pace/pauses) → human review of emotion and stress → mix (see audio-sound rules: -14 LUFS, compression from -6dB).
- Multilingual localization: AI dubbing + lip-sync (see caption-localization).
- AI music for beat-sync: parameterized prompts (mood/instruments/tempo/mode) + commercial compliance check.
- Compliance triple-check: voice cloning (consent/license/disclosure), music (human contribution/terms/distribution policy), labeling (AI content declaration).
